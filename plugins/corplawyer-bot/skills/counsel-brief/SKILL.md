# Counsel brief

Prepare a brief for the actual decision and intended reader. Use existing
findings and source material; if the source agreement or research is missing,
state the gap rather than presenting a complete review. Do not repeat intake
questions that have already been answered.

Include the requested decision and relevant matter/document version, leading
issues in priority order, source locations, options or proposed revisions, and
one recommended next action with its reason. Keep the following distinguishable
where they occur:

- Supplied facts, attributed to their source.
- Contract language, with clause/page references.
- Current researched law or guidance, with official citations and dates.
- Assumptions and the effect if they are wrong.
- Unknowns and focused questions for counsel or the owner.

Use plain language. Explain which conclusions are supported by the document
alone and which remain legal research questions. Avoid universal “compliant”,
“safe to sign” or “enforceable” conclusions when the evidence does not establish
them. Identify actual deadlines from supplied or verified sources; no invented
urgent deadlines or blanket requirement for counsel for every ordinary task.

End with readiness stated against the user's goal: complete for this bounded
review, draft with listed gaps, or blocked by a specific missing input/capability.
A proposed negotiation or counsel handoff remains a draft until the user
explicitly authorizes any external send. For authorized retention or later
resumption, use [Allura continuity](../allura-continuity/SKILL.md).
